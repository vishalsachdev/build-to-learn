Dear Professor Eaton, and co-editors,

I am writing about your Journal of University Teaching & Learning Practice special issue, "Postplagiarism and Generativism: Human–AI Hybrid Approaches." I realize the submission window closed on 20 May; I would be grateful to know whether late consideration is possible, or whether a follow-on is planned, as the call fits my work closely.

The paper reframes the integrity question from "did AI do it?" to "where does the student's thinking show?" It argues that generative AI makes building cheap, so learning — and accountability — relocate to judgment: framing a build, comparing and justifying among AI-generated variants, and reflecting in one's own voice. I frame this as cognitive apprenticeship (Collins, Brown & Newman, 1989) scaled by AI, and address assessment integrity directly, including the limits of viva-style defenses as student-facing avatars mature. It is roughly 6,000 words, conceptual, with worked cases from business education.

If the issue is fully closed, I would welcome any pointer to a suitable home. I am happy to share the full draft.
