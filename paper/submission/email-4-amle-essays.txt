Dear Professor Harley,

AMLE invites authors to explore Essay ideas with you before submitting, so I am writing to ask whether the following would suit the Essays section. The provocation: generative AI has collapsed the cost of producing a working artifact, so "Create" — long treated as the culmination of learning — becomes an entry move. The hard cognitive work moves downstream, to judgment, iteration, and taste. I formalize this as a five-step build → compare → reflect loop, argue that it is cognitive apprenticeship (Collins, Brown & Newman, 1989) scaled by AI, and develop the implications for management education: a "BuilderProf" faculty stance, authentic assessment that weights process over artifact, the equity of AI fluency as the new digital divide, and the relationships a community of builders forms — the durable, AI-resistant layer of a business school.

It is disciplined provocation grounded in worked cases, roughly 6,000 words, and explicit about which claims are evidenced and which are offered as bets. If it sounds like a fit, I would be glad to share the full draft.
