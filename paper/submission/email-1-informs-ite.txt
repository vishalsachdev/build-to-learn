Dear Dr. Creemers,

I am the Academic Director of the MS in Business Analytics at the Gies College of Business, University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign. I am preparing a conceptual paper and would like to confirm fit with Transactions on Education before submitting.

The paper argues that generative AI has collapsed the cost of producing a working artifact, which inverts a standing assumption in learning design: "Create" — the top of the revised Bloom taxonomy — becomes an entry move rather than a culmination. It formalizes the shift as a five-step build → compare → reflect loop, best understood as cognitive apprenticeship (Collins, Brown & Newman, 1989) scaled by AI, and grounds the argument in worked cases from analytics and management education while addressing assessment integrity, equity, and faculty development. It is a position/conceptual piece of roughly 6,000 words, explicit about which claims are evidenced and which are offered as hypotheses.

I noticed the journal's 2025 special issue on Generative AI in Teaching Management Science, OR, OM, and Analytics, guest-edited by Jeroen Beliën, Dries Goossens, Juan Jaramillo, and Kyle Maclean. Two questions: (1) is a successor or follow-on issue planned, and (2) absent that, would a conceptual paper of this kind be welcome via regular submission? I would be glad to share the full draft.
